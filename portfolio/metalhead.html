IV ✶ The Design

Why this works

Pyre is not a real band. It is a controlled test of whether a traditional blackletter system can run an entire site, at full aggression, without losing a single reader.

Textura, load-bearing

Two authentic blackletters do the work: Maguntia for the wordmark, the heavier Cook for every heading, track, city, and product. Body copy runs in Grenze, the rough-cut roman companion, so the page reads as one hand rather than a costume over a neutral sans.

The legibility floor

Blackletter never sets below about 19px, never sets in all caps, and never carries a full paragraph. Every micro-label, price, date, and button is mono. That single rule is what buys the rest of the aggression.

Distortion in the render, not the asset

The wordmark and sigil pass through turbulence displacement filters, so the roughness is generated at paint time. Nothing is a pre-distressed image, nothing pixelates, and the type stays selectable text.

Damaged emulsion

Three fixed layers over the whole page: coarse blotch, fine grain, and 1px scanline degradation. The band seams are crosshatched scratches and the newsprint band is torn along both edges rather than cut.

Hierarchy by temperature

Rank is heat. Flare at the wordmark, hot core on labels and calls to action, deep ember on ordinals, ash on support. Grounds sit at near-absolute black so the fire is the only light source in the room.

Contrast as a structural beat

After three black sections the merch band inverts to soot-stained newsprint. It lands like a snare hit, and it proves the palette survives on light ground, which is where every printed piece will live.
